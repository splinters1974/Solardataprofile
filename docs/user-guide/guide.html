If you only read one page

Read section 1. Then, before you send anything to a client, check three things on every site: the calendar is right and ticked as confirmed, the Data review tab has nothing left held back that matters, and the data quality verdict isn't Poor. Most wrong answers come from one of those.

Standard and Advanced

There are two versions, built from the same engine, so the same file gives the same numbers in both. Standard (Ameresco-Data-Analyser-Standard.html, grey badge) is for quick screening: Overview, Data review, Calendar, Heatmap, Capacity, Charts and Settings, with findings and reports. Advanced (Ameresco-Data-Analyser-Advanced.html, green badge) adds Base load, Unusual days, Savings scenarios, the Opportunities and Estate comparison views, opportunity tracking, project files, the week-by-week explorer and the carbon panel. This guide covers Advanced; everything in Standard works the same way. The badge shows next to the name, on the customer password page and in every PDF footer.

What changed in the Advanced version

Missing readings are no longer counted as zero. Annual figures are only shown with enough data. Calendars have per-day hours, overnight shifts, closures and exceptions. Findings carry separate impact and confidence and can be tracked as opportunities. New tabs for data review, base load, heatmap, unusual days, savings scenarios and capacity. Time-band tariffs, an optional carbon factor, estate comparison, and project files you can save and reopen. Expect some figures to differ from version 1, mainly where files had gaps.

1. Quick start

  1. Open the app. Double-click Ameresco-Data-Analyser-Advanced.html. It opens in Chrome or Edge. Nothing installs and nothing goes online.
  2. Load the meter files. Drag them onto the upload box. One file per meter; each becomes a site. Loading the same file twice is refused.
  3. Set the calendars. Every site starts as Office / commercial with preset hours marked unconfirmed. Use the blue Set building type and hours for all sites button above the totals (it shows how many sites are still unconfirmed), then open each site's Calendar tab, correct the hours, add closures (school holidays, shutdowns) and tick Hours confirmed with the site.
  4. Check the data. On each site's Data review tab, read how the file was read, decide on any held-back days (clock changes, duplicate rows) and exclude anything you know is wrong.
  5. Set the rate. A flat default rate applies everywhere (25p/kWh). Override it on a site, or enter time bands. Enter a carbon factor if you want carbon figures; there is no default.
  6. Read the results. Where to start ranks the sites. Opportunities lists every finding across the estate. Click a site for its tabs.
  7. Save and export. Save project keeps everything (data, settings, statuses, scenarios) in one file to reopen later. Then export PDFs or Excel, or make a customer edition.
Before anything goes to a client
  • Every calendar is checked and confirmed. Unconfirmed hours cap closed-hours findings at Medium confidence.
  • The data quality verdict is Good or Check, and you've read the Check items. Don't quote figures from a Poor file.
  • Annual figures say indicative under 330 days of valid readings and don't appear at all under 180 (unless you forced them, which shows a warning).
  • Costs are associated costs: what a pattern costs now at the rates entered. Not savings. Scenario figures are what-ifs. Keep your covering email consistent.

2. What the tool is, and what it isn't

The Data Analyser turns half-hourly (HH) electricity data into an evidence-based list of where a site or estate uses energy when it shouldn't, priced at your rates, with a check on spare supply capacity. It handles one site or hundreds and produces Ameresco-branded PDFs and spreadsheets.

It is

  • A screening and investigation tool. It shows where to look, how much it matters and how good the evidence is.
  • Offline and private. Everything runs in your browser. Files are read on your laptop and never uploaded.
  • Transparent. Every finding has a Show calculation and evidence link with the method, sample sizes and dates. The formulas are in section 16.

It isn't

  • An equipment diagnosis. Whole-site HH data shows when demand happens, not which plant causes it. Checks name possible explanations to confirm on site.
  • Verified savings. Associated costs and scenario figures are estimates. Measurement and verification needs a proper baseline and adjustment.
  • Weather normalised. Comparisons between periods are of measured demand. A cold winter will show as more use.
  • Live monitoring. It analyses the files you load, nothing more.
  • A connection study or bill validation. Headroom is from the peaks in the data. Fixed and capacity charges aren't modelled.

Where the data goes

Nowhere. Closing the tab clears it; Clear all data does the same without closing. Project files, PDFs, spreadsheets and customer editions you export are ordinary files in Downloads, so look after them in line with the client's data agreement. Project files contain the half-hourly data and your internal notes.

Browser and versions

Use current Chrome or Edge. The app is one file of about 2.3 MB and doesn't update itself; replace your copy when a new version comes out. The Standard and Advanced versions are separate files and can sit side by side. Project files record their format version, so a later version of the app can tell what it is reading.

3. Loading meter data

Files and layouts

  • File types: .xlsx, .xlsm, .xls, .csv, .txt. Comma, semicolon or tab separated.
  • One meter per file. The file name becomes the site name. A meter reference (MPAN or serial) is picked up if the file has one, and a warning shows if two files carry the same meter.

How readings are treated

  • Missing is not zero. Blank cells, text (error codes) and absent days are missing. They're left out of every figure and shown hatched on the heatmap. Annual figures scale from valid hours only.
  • Units. Readings are taken as kWh per half hour unless the headings say kW, in which case they're converted. Check the unit on the Data review tab.
  • Start or end labels. Whether a column labelled 00:30 means the half hour starting or ending then is detected from the headings and shown. Change it in Settings if the supplier says otherwise.
  • Clock changes. A spring day with 46 readings and an autumn day with 50 are read correctly. A clock-change day with 48 readings can't be placed safely, so it's held back until you review it.
  • Duplicate rows. Identical repeats are counted once. Two different rows for the same date are both kept and the day is held back until you choose which to use.
  • Negative readings are a fault on an import meter, but normal on export or net meters. Set the meter type in Settings.

What it refuses

A file with several meters stacked one after another (lots of conflicting repeated dates) is refused rather than spliced into one profile. Split it into one file per MPAN.

4. Data review

Each site's Data review tab shows what the app did with the file and lets you correct it. Nothing you do here changes the original file; it's all reversible.

Held back for review

Clock-change days that couldn't be placed and dates with conflicting duplicate rows. For each, keep it (or pick the first or second row) or leave it out. Until you decide, it's left out.

Exclusions

Leave out readings you know are wrong: a meter fault, a period of works, estimated data. Give a date range, optionally a time window, and a reason. Excluded readings are left out of every figure, shown pink on the heatmap, and listed in the Excel and PDF appendix.

Data quality check

Every file is checked and given a verdict: Good, Check (usable, read the flagged items) or Poor (fix the data before quoting figures). Each check lists its dates with buttons to exclude them, mark them as fine, or inspect them.

5. Operating calendar

The calendar says when the site is open, half hour by half hour. Every closed-hours figure depends on it, so it's the most important setting.

  • Weekly hours. Each day has its own hours and can have several periods (e.g. 07:00 to 12:00 and 13:00 to 19:00). A period that closes at or before it opens runs overnight: Monday 22:00 to 06:00 covers Tuesday morning.
  • Bank holidays. Choose England and Wales, Scotland or Northern Ireland, then closed, Sunday hours or normal hours. Untick a date to treat it as a normal day, or add a local holiday.
  • Dated closures. School holidays, Christmas shutdowns: the site is closed all day. School term dates are never assumed from the building type; enter them here or paste several at once (one per line, e.g. 24/12/2025 - 02/01/2026 Christmas).
  • Exceptional periods. Dates with different hours: events, extended opening, works.
  • Low-activity window (24-hour sites only). A hospital or data centre never closes, so there is no "demand when shut" to measure. Enter its quietest hours (say 01:00 to 05:00) and the app works out a night-time reference from them, shown as the second reference on the Base load tab and usable in Savings scenario A. It doesn't mark those hours as closed or create closed-hours findings. Leave it off for sites that close.
  • Hours confirmed. Building-type presets are a starting point. Until you tick this, calendar-dependent findings are capped at Medium confidence and the site shows unconfirmed.

The bulk setting on the main page replaces every site's weekly hours and holiday rules, and adds any closures entered there to each site's own.

6. Site settings

Rates and time bands

A flat rate in p/kWh, fully delivered. Or switch to time bands: each band has days, a time window and a rate (e.g. weekday day, night, weekend). The app checks that every half hour has exactly one band; if not, it lists the problem and uses the flat rate until it's fixed. Bank holidays can be priced as Sundays. Fixed and capacity charges aren't included and the reports say so.

Carbon

Enter a factor in kgCO2e/kWh, the reporting year and the source on the main page, or click one of the suggested UK Government factors to fill all three: 2026: 0.13096 and 2025: 0.177 kgCO2e/kWh (location-based Scope 2, generation only; transmission and distribution losses are a separate Scope 3 factor). Use the factor for the year you are reporting, and check it against gov.uk before formal reporting. Nothing is applied by default, so no carbon is shown until a factor is entered. It's kWh × one annual factor: it doesn't show any benefit from moving load in time.

Site details

  • Floor area (m²) switches on kWh/m² in the estate comparison.
  • Physical site and role. Meters with the same physical site name are grouped. Mark each as main, submeter or separate. Submeters are never added to their main meter, in the site group or in estate totals.
  • Meter type (import, export, net) decides whether negative readings are a fault.

7. The portfolio view

The main page shows estate totals (observed kWh over each meter's own period, annual cost for meters that can be annualised, submeters excluded), then three views.

Where to start

Sites are ranked by the associated annual cost of closed-hours use above the reference: energy used when the building should be shut, beyond what never switches off. It's usually the cheapest thing to tackle, so it's a good first sort. Meters with too little data to annualise are listed after, unranked. Columns show valid days and coverage, observed kWh, annual kWh and cost (estimates), base kW, closed-hours share, the ranking figure, firm headroom, the data verdict and finding counts.

Opportunities

Use it as the action list for a client: sort by impact or confidence, filter to what's not yet done, and set statuses as you go (section 8). The total shown is not additive, because findings on one site overlap.

Estate comparison

Raw consumption doesn't show which site is better run, so the comparison is fair or it isn't shown: same building type, at least 90 shared days, complete days only, and kWh/m² where floor areas are entered, plus kWh per scheduled open hour. Nothing is labelled best or worst. The sample sites cover different years, so they share no days, which is why the table above is empty.

8. Findings and opportunities

Each site has ten tabs on a bar that stays pinned to the top of the window as you scroll, so you can always jump to another one. The active tab is filled blue; a small badge flags something to look at (high findings, a data check, a calendar to confirm, the number of unusual days or active scenarios).

Where the energy goes splits observed consumption three ways, which add up to the total: base load (up to the reference in every half hour: what never switches off), open hours above it, and the closed-hours excess above it, which is the ranking figure. It also appears in the PDF appendix. The carbon panel shows tCO2e at the entered factor, the share from closed-hours excess and what the active savings scenarios would remove. Every chart has a download button (green arrow) that saves it as a picture for proposals.

A site's Overview tab shows its key figures (each tagged Measured or Estimate), its top three investigations and all findings.

Impact and confidence are separate

  • Impact is how much it could matter: High at 10% or more of the site's energy cost, Medium at 4%, Low below. Some findings have a fixed level.
  • Confidence is how good the evidence is. It starts High and is capped by: Poor data (Low) or Check data (Medium); under 180 valid days (Low) or under 330 (Medium); unconfirmed hours (Medium, for calendar-dependent findings); a small sample. The reasons are listed in the evidence.

There's no combined score. The top three are the highest impact, then the best evidence.

Tracking

Each finding is an opportunity record with a status (New, Under review, Site visit required, Confirmed, Action agreed, Implemented, Closed), an owner, a date and notes. These save with the project. Customer editions show status but leave out owner and notes unless you choose to include them.

9. Base load and closed hours

The app shows two references and says which one each figure uses.

  • All-period reference: the 5th percentile of every valid half hour. The load that is almost always there. Shown next to the lowest single half hour so you can see they differ.
  • Closed-hours reference: the 5th percentile of valid closed half hours only, with the sample size. Better for sites that run plant overnight on purpose.

Closed-hours excess for each valid closed half hour is max(0, kWh − reference kW × 0.5 h), priced at that half hour's rate, summed, then scaled to a year if the site is annualised.

Did a change make a difference? (before and after)

Use it when you know something changed on site on a date: a new BMS or time-clock schedule, plant switched off, an LED or controls upgrade. Enter the date and a window (2 to 12 weeks, 8 by default). The app compares the weeks before with the same number of weeks after: average demand, base load and average demand when closed. It then says in one line what moved and what it would be worth over a year at the site's average rate if it held.

Keep the window short so the seasons don't swamp the answer: a change made in November will look like a rise just because winter started. It shows what changed in the meter data, not why.

10. Heatmap and days

The fastest way to read a site. Look for dark bands outside the orange marks, weekends that look like weekdays, holidays that don't show, step changes and gaps. Hover for the reading; click a day to inspect it.

The reference profile is the median kW in each half hour across the previous 8 comparable days (same weekday and calendar class, complete, no unresolved data question). The number of days and their range are shown. Below it, the week view shows seven days as a grid.

Compare periods

Answers "is the site using more or less than before?". Pick a period (quick picks: last 4 weeks, 3 months, 6 months) and the app compares it with the period just before it and with the same weeks a year earlier, in one sentence each. Use it to show a customer how a recent month compares, to check that savings are holding, or to spot a site creeping up. The year-earlier comparison is the fairer one because it is the same season. Only days complete in both periods are counted; if too few match, it says so rather than guess.

Week by week (Advanced)

Below the heatmap, a bar per week (kWh or £). Click any bar to see that week half hour by half hour, with closed hours shaded, the result of active savings scenarios, and, when time bands are set, the unit rate in each half hour underneath. It opens on the highest week.

Daily load profile (Charts tab)

The quickest way to see how consistent a site is: where the lines bunch, the site behaves the same every day; a second band means a different kind of day (here, weekends); stray lines are one-offs. Filter by days (all, open, closed, weekdays, weekends) and month. In Advanced, switch to With scenarios to see the effect of the active savings scenarios against the measured average.

The Charts tab keeps the earlier analyser charts (day-of-week profile, load duration curve, day and night by month, week comparison, every reading). Its date filter affects those charts only; findings always use the full record.

11. Unusual days

Each day is compared with the previous 8 comparable days: same actual weekday and the same calendar class (so a bank holiday Monday is compared with bank holidays, not Mondays). At least 6 are needed; days without enough are counted as cannot apply. Days with unresolved data problems are kept out of the baseline.

A day is flagged when it's more than 25% from the baseline median and more than 4 × the scaled median absolute deviation. High and low days are both flagged. A low day is not a saving until the data and an operational change are confirmed.

Persistent changes

A sustained shift rather than a one-off: demand at least 15% above or below its baseline on 5 of 7 consecutive eligible days. Reported once with the date it started.

All these thresholds can be changed in the site's Settings.

12. Savings scenarios

What-if estimates applied to one copy of the half-hourly data, in the order listed. Each scenario works on what earlier ones left, so nothing is counted twice, and no half hour goes below its retained floor or below zero. Every input starts at zero.

ScenarioWhat it does
A. Reduce closed-hours excessRemoves a share of demand above the reference in every closed half hour. The reference (the load that never switches off) stays, so even 100% leaves that level running overnight and at weekends. Add B to test cutting it.
B. Reduce base load in chosen hoursRemoves a fixed kW in a time window on chosen days, never below the floor.
C. Remove early-start or late-stop demandRemoves a share of demand above a floor in windows around opening and closing.
D. Lower peak demandUsually Hold demand at or below a target kW: every half hour above it is brought down to it. One click sets the target to 90%, 80% or 70% of the current peak, and the result shows how many half hours on how many days that touches. You can also trim the N highest half hours or every half hour above a level. Remove takes the energy away; shift moves it to a chosen window the same day. Expect a small energy saving: the value of lower peaks is in capacity and peak-time network charges (not priced here) and electrification headroom.

The chart shows the average day before and after. Switch between all days, days the site opens and closed days (weekends, holidays, closures). "All days" mixes the two, so daytime still shows working-day use even when closed-hours demand is cut; "closed days" shows scenario A clearly, with demand brought down to the dashed reference line.

Check figure

10 kW removed for 12 hours a day on 100 days is 12,000 kWh. At 25p/kWh that's £3,000. The app is tested against this.

Scenarios save with the project and appear in the Excel export (with an interval sheet for internal use) and the PDF appendix. Customer editions show them read-only.

13. Capacity

Enter the agreed supply capacity (kVA), power factor and safety margin on the Capacity tab.

The chart opens on the week of the highest half hour; step through weeks with the arrows. Then the ten highest half hours, one per day:

  • kW vs kVA. Half-hourly data gives kW (a half-hour average, not an instantaneous maximum). kVA = kW ÷ power factor, which is an assumption you enter (0.95 if unknown).
  • Recurrence. How many half hours, on how many days, came within 10% of the peak. One-off peaks are easier to manage than recurring ones.
  • Firm headroom = (usable kVA − peak kVA) × power factor, where usable kVA is the agreed capacity less the margin (10% default).
  • Test a new load (heat pumps, EV charging): enter kW, hours and season. The result says whether it would exceed usable capacity in the analysed half hours under the entered assumptions.
Wording

"No exceedance" means none in the analysed half hours under the entered assumptions. A colder year, load on other meters or DNO constraints can change the answer. It's a screen before a capacity conversation, not a substitute.

17. Limits and troubleshooting

Known limits

  • No fixed or capacity charges in costs. Time bands cover unit rates only.
  • No weather normalisation, so year-on-year and before-and-after comparisons include weather.
  • Timestamped files: after a project is saved and reopened, cells that held text show as blank (still missing, so the figures don't change).
  • Large estates work but slow down; load in batches if the browser struggles.
